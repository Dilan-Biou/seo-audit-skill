# What is this thing? (the friendly version)

You built a website. It looks great. But when people search Google for what you do… you're
nowhere. That's an **SEO** problem — SEO just means "helping Google find, understand and
show your site to the right people".

Fixing SEO usually means going through a long checklist of boring, easy-to-forget details.
**This skill does that checklist for you**, using an AI coding assistant (Claude).

---

## Think of it like a car inspection 🚗

You bring your car to a good mechanic. They:

1. **Inspect everything** and write down what's wrong.
2. **Ask you a few questions** they can't answer alone — "how far do you drive?", "do you
   want the cheap tires or the good ones?"
3. **Fix it all** while you get coffee.
4. **Hand you the keys** with a report: what they fixed, and what _you_ still need to do
   (like "come back in a month for a check-up").

That's exactly how this skill works — just for websites.

---

## How it works, step by step

### 1. It looks at everything 🔍

The assistant goes through your website's code and your live site, and checks things like:

- Can Google actually read your pages?
- Does every page have a clear title, like a book has a title on its cover?
- Is the site fast on a phone with a weak connection?
- When someone shares your link in WhatsApp or Telegram, does a nice preview show up?
- Does the text on your site say things that are actually true?

It writes everything it finds in a report.

### 2. It asks you questions — but only once 🙋

Some things only _you_ know: Is your app free? Does it work on iPhone? Who made it? What
words do your customers type into Google?

Instead of interrupting you fifty times, it puts **all** its questions in **one file**.
You answer them once, at your own pace. If you skip a question, it uses a safe default
instead of guessing.

### 3. It fixes things 🛠️

It goes through the list and fixes what can be fixed in the code — and after each fix it
**tests** that the site still works, on a phone-sized screen and a computer screen.

It **doesn't save the changes for good** on its own. You (or your developer) look at what
it did first, and then decide to keep it.

### 4. It hands you a to-do list ✅

Some things can't be done in code — like telling Google "hey, I updated my site" or adding
your website link to your app store page. The skill gives you a simple list of those, with
dates for when to check back.

---

## Things it will never do 🚫

- **Make things up.** It won't write "works offline!" if your app doesn't. It asks first.
- **Fake reviews or star ratings.** Ever.
- **Use your competitors' names** to steal their searches.
- **Touch your servers or accounts.** Anything outside the code goes on your to-do list.

---

## Why does it exist?

It was built while fixing the SEO of a real, live website. Along the way we hit a lot of
sneaky problems: Google remembering an old copy of the site, a page that looked fine on a computer
but hid the most important text on phones, a download link that said "not found" when the
server was just temporarily down…

Every one of those lessons went into this skill, so the next website doesn't have to learn
them the hard way.

## Quick questions

**Will my site jump to #1 on Google tomorrow?**
No — nobody can promise that honestly. Google takes **days to weeks** to notice changes.
What this does is remove everything that's _holding you back_, so your site has a fair
chance.

**Do I need to be a programmer?**
Someone needs to run the AI assistant and look over the changes — usually a developer.
But the questions and the to-do list are written for normal humans.

**Does it work for any website?**
It works best for sites built with a popular tool called **Next.js**, and it has general
guidance for everything else.

**Is it free?**
Yes, it's open source (MIT license). You need an AI coding assistant to run it.
